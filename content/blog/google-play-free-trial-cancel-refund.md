Waking up to a `GOOGLE *` or `GOOGLE *SERVICES` charge on your bank card after a 3-day or 7-day app trial is frustrating—especially when your card statement hides which app triggered the charge and uninstalling the app from your Android phone did nothing to stop it.

To fix it without risking another renewal or an account lock, follow three steps in order: confirm which Google Account holds the receipt, cancel auto-renew inside Google Play so it cannot charge next cycle, and submit a refund request based on how many hours have passed since billing.

## Step 0: Identify which app (and Google Account) charged you

Many Android users sign into two or three Gmail addresses across work, personal, and older devices. Subscriptions belong strictly to the Google Account that was active when you tapped **Subscribe**.

| Where to look | Exact path | What it reveals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Google Payments Center** | [payments.google.com](https://payments.google.com) → **Activity** or **Subscriptions & services** | Shows the exact app name behind `GOOGLE *` card charges |
| **Gmail / email search** | Search `Google Play Order Receipt` or `Your Google Play order` | Shows the `GPA.xxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxxx` order number needed for refunds |
| **Android Play Store** | Open **Play Store** → tap top-right **profile icon** → switch accounts if needed | Reveals whether the trial sits under a secondary Gmail address |
| **Desktop / iPhone browser** | Open [play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions](https://play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions) | Lets you inspect and cancel subscriptions even if you no longer own an Android phone |

If none of your Google Accounts show an order receipt for the charge, check whether the app took your card directly on its website, billed through [PayPal automatic payments](https://steadyrenew.com/blog/paypal-automatic-payments-cancel-find/), or actually billed via [Apple’s App Store](https://steadyrenew.com/blog/apple-free-trial-charge-refund/).

## Step 1: Cancel the subscription immediately

Requesting a refund does not always halt future renewals on its own. Cancel the subscription first so another billing cycle cannot slip through while a refund decision is pending.

1. Open the **Google Play Store** app (or visit [play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions](https://play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions) in any browser).
2. Check the top-right **profile icon** to confirm you are on the billed Google Account.
3. Tap **Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions**.
4. Select the app that just charged you and tap **Cancel subscription**.
5. Pick a reason when prompted and confirm cancellation.

Once canceled, the subscription entry updates to show when access expires rather than a next billing date. Google’s official instructions (including pausing options offered by some apps) are listed in [Google Play’s cancel subscription help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7018481).

## Step 2: Request a Google Play refund (within 48 hours vs. after)

Google Play splits subscription refund handling by timing:

- **Under 48 hours since the charge:** Request the refund directly through Google Play’s self-service portal at [support.google.com/googleplay/workflow/9813244](https://support.google.com/googleplay/workflow/9813244) (Google’s official **Request a Google Play refund** flow). Sign in with the exact Google Account that made the purchase, select the `GPA.*` order from the list, choose the reason that fits honestly (such as purchasing by mistake or forgetting a trial renewal), and submit.
- **More than 48 hours after the charge:** Google’s automated self-service window generally closes after 48 hours. At that point, contact the **app developer directly** using the developer email listed on the app’s Play Store page under **App support**, and include your `GPA.` order number and purchase date.

### Typical Google Play refund timelines

Google usually emails a decision within **1 to 4 business days** (often within 15 minutes for straightforward recent requests). Once approved, how fast money returns depends on your payment method:

| Payment method | Typical refund clearing window |
| --- | --- |
| **Google Play balance** | 1 business day |
| **PayPal** | 3–5 business days (sometimes up to 10 days) |
| **Credit or debit card** | 3–5 business days (up to 10 business days depending on bank) |
| **Direct carrier / mobile billing** | 1–30 business days depending on carrier statement cycle |

You can review Google’s full eligibility policy and payment-method timelines on [Google Play’s refund policy page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2479637).

**Avoid filing a bank chargeback first.** Unless a charge is genuinely unauthorized fraud you cannot resolve with Google, disputing a valid `GOOGLE *` purchase directly with your card issuer can cause Google Payments to suspend the payment profile on that Google Account until resolved.

## How to stop Google Play trials from sneaking past you

- **Cancel right after starting most trials:** On Google Play, canceling a third-party free trial minutes after signing up usually lets you keep testing the app until the final day of the trial while immediately removing auto-renew risk. Always read the confirmation prompt first in case a specific promotion ends access early.
- **Set trial reminders 2 to 3 days early:** Treating the final day as your deadline leaves almost zero margin for time-zone differences or early billing attempts.
- **Keep active trials in one neutral list:** A calendar alert works well for an occasional trial. ## FAQ

### Why did deleting the Android app not cancel my Google Play free trial?

Deleting an app only removes its software from your phone or tablet. Your trial contract lives on your Google Account under **Play Store → Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions**, where billing continues until you tap **Cancel subscription**.

### What if I was billed for Google Play on an iPhone or after switching away from Android?

You do not need an Android phone to cancel. Open [play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions](https://play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions) in Safari or Chrome on any phone or computer, sign into the billed Google Account, and cancel from the web.

### Can I get a refund if I forgot to cancel a Google Play trial after 48 hours?

Google Play’s instant web refund workflow usually covers the first 48 hours after a charge. Past 48 hours, your best option is emailing the app developer (found under **App support** on the app’s Play Store listing) with your `GPA.` order ID and explaining promptly that you meant to cancel the trial.

### Why is my Google Play charge missing from Payments & subscriptions?

The two most common reasons are being signed into a second Gmail account in the Play Store, or the subscription having been billed directly on the developer’s website (for example via credit card or PayPal) rather than through Google Play’s in-app billing.

### Does canceling a refunded Google Play subscription let me keep using it until month-end?

Usually no. When you cancel an ordinary paid subscription without a refund, access continues until the end of the period you already paid for. Once a refund is approved, paid access is revoked right away.
