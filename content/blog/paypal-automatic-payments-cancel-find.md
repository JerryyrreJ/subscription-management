Many people discover forgotten software, hosting, or streaming renewals not on a credit card statement, but as a vague line item reading `PAYPAL *MERCHANT` days after the money has already left checking. Worse, replacing an expired debit card rarely stops recurring payments on PayPal—PayPal simply falls back to your linked bank account or secondary card.

To stop a PayPal payment from billing you again, revoke the merchant’s authorization inside PayPal’s **Manage automatic payments** settings. Checking that list takes under five minutes on desktop or mobile and lets you review, update, or cancel recurring PayPal payments directly at the wallet level.

## Why recurring PayPal payments survive a new card

When you check out with PayPal for a subscription or free trial, you sign a merchant **billing agreement** (PayPal calls these **automatic payments**). Unlike a one-time card swipe:

- The merchant charges your PayPal account on schedule, and PayPal pulls from your primary funding source or backup card automatically if the primary card expires.
- Deleting the app or closing a tab never revokes the billing agreement.
- Even if a merchant’s own website hides the cancel button behind customer-support chat, canceling the automatic payment inside PayPal blocks future PayPal-routed pulls from that authorization.

## How to manage and stop PayPal automatic payments on desktop web

Using a desktop browser gives you the clearest view of active merchants, funding methods, and past transaction logs:

1. Sign in at `paypal.com` and click the **Settings** gear icon near the top right.
2. Select **Payments** in the secondary navigation bar (or open your account’s **Manage automatic payments** section).
3. Click **Automatic payments** (labeled **Manage preapproved payments** in some older regional interfaces).
4. Review the **Active** tab on the left. Click any merchant name to inspect the billing details, agreement ID, and assigned payment method.
5. Click **Cancel** (or **Cancel automatic billing**), then confirm when prompted.

PayPal moves that merchant from **Active** to **Inactive** immediately, stopping future recurring pulls under that agreement. If you want to keep a subscription active on PayPal but switch which card or bank funds it, edit the **Payment method** on that same merchant screen instead of canceling. Official steps are documented in [PayPal’s automatic payments help page](https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/what-is-an-automatic-payment-and-how-do-i-update-or-cancel-one-ts1440).

## How to find and stop recurring payments in the PayPal app

If you only have your phone handy, the mobile app exposes the same **Automatic payments** list under your Wallet tab:

1. Open the **PayPal** app and tap **Wallet** at the bottom right.
2. Scroll down to **Manage** or tap **Autopay** / **Automatic payments**.
3. Tap the merchant you no longer want to fund.
4. Tap **Remove PayPal as your payment method** or **Cancel automatic payment** and confirm.

Menu wording occasionally shifts across iOS, Android, and country regions—if you do not see **Autopay** inside Wallet, tap your **Profile icon → Payments → Automatic payments** instead.

## What canceling in PayPal does (and does not) do

| Action | Stops PayPal from paying future bills? | Ends your merchant account or contract? | Refunds an already-posted charge? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cancel in PayPal Automatic Payments | Yes, for that PayPal billing agreement | Not automatically—check merchant terms | No |
| Cancel on the merchant’s website | Usually, once confirmed | Yes | Only if merchant policy allows |
| Delete merchant app from phone | No | No | No |

Two guardrails save headaches later:

- **Cancel on the merchant’s side too when you have a contract.** Revoking PayPal funding stops PayPal from honoring the pull, yet some annual software, telecom, or gym memberships still expect formal cancellation in their billing portal (otherwise they may email past-due notices or try a backup card you gave them directly). Cancel with the merchant first when easy, and kill the PayPal agreement as your safety lock.
- **Act separately if a charge already posted.** Canceling automatic payments stops *future* cycles; it does not reverse yesterday’s payment. If an unwanted renewal already went through, check the merchant’s refund policy first or review PayPal’s Resolution Center options for unauthorized or duplicate billing.

## Check PayPal alongside App Store and Google Play

PayPal is often used as the **underlying funding method** inside Apple Subscriptions or Google Play. If your bank statement shows `PAYPAL *APPLE` or `PAYPAL *GOOGLE`, canceling inside PayPal alone can trigger failed-payment warnings on your Apple Account or Google Account—or make Apple/Google pause iCloud storage until resolved.

Whenever the descriptor names Apple or Google, open **iPhone Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions** (or follow our [Apple charge check and refund guide](https://steadyrenew.com/blog/apple-free-trial-charge-refund/)) or [Google Play’s subscription cancel & refund steps](https://steadyrenew.com/blog/google-play-free-trial-cancel-refund/) first so the store stops billing cleanly.

## Keep annual PayPal renewals from sneaking back up

PayPal handles a surprising share of annual renewals: web domains, VPNs, WordPress plugins, Patreon tiers, and indie tools billed once every twelve months. ## FAQ

### Where did "Preapproved payments" go in PayPal?

PayPal renamed "Preapproved payments" to **Automatic payments** (or **Autopay** in parts of the mobile app). You can find the full list under **Settings → Payments → Automatic payments** on the web or **Wallet → Automatic payments** on mobile.

### How do I reactivate an automatic payment on PayPal?

You cannot reactivate a canceled automatic payment inside PayPal—once a billing agreement moves to **Inactive**, PayPal does not let you flip it back to **Active**. To resume recurring PayPal billing with that merchant, return to the merchant’s billing or checkout page and authorize PayPal again as your payment method, which creates a new active agreement.

### Does canceling an automatic payment in PayPal refund my last charge?

No. Canceling an automatic payment only revokes permission for future recurring charges under that billing agreement. For a charge that already cleared, contact the merchant to request a refund according to their terms, or open a dispute in PayPal’s Resolution Center if the payment was unauthorized or billed after confirmed cancellation.

### Can a merchant still charge me after I cancel PayPal automatic payments?

PayPal stops honoring new pulls under that specific PayPal billing agreement once it shows as **Inactive**. However, if you previously saved a credit card directly on the merchant’s own website—or signed up under a second PayPal email—the merchant could still bill that separate payment method unless you also cancel in the merchant’s billing portal.

### Why didn’t a specific subscription show up in my PayPal Automatic Payments list?

Check the **Inactive** filter in case it was already canceled earlier, verify whether you used a different PayPal account, or look at your PayPal transaction activity to see if the payment was processed as a one-time checkout or through Apple/Google Play billing.

### Should I remove my credit card from PayPal instead of canceling automatic payments?

No. Removing one card often causes PayPal to route the scheduled charge to your linked checking account, PayPal balance, or backup card instead. Always cancel the specific merchant inside **Automatic payments** directly.
