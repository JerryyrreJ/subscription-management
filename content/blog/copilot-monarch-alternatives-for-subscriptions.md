Copilot Money and Monarch Money are built to be complete personal finance hubs: checking accounts, credit cards, net worth, investment balances, and category budgets all in one feed. If you want automated cash-flow budgeting across US or Canadian banks, both tools earn their $95 to $100+ annual subscription.

If all you actually open the app for is seeing upcoming recurring charges, trials, and annual software renewals, full-suite finance trackers add cost and bank-connection friction you do not need. Dropping bank aggregators gives you simpler alternatives that cost $0 or a small one-time fee.

## When a full finance app is overkill for subscriptions

Three common pain points push people away from all-in-one budgeting suites:

- **You pay an annual subscription just to avoid forgotten subscriptions.** Spending $95 to $100 every year to watch $40 to $150/month of SaaS, streaming, or AI tools takes a big bite out of what you save by canceling one unused plan.
- **Bank connectors drop or miss non-US cards.** Plaid and Finicity integrations frequently require two-factor re-authentication, lag on pending charges, or skip Alipay, WeChat Pay, Wise, and regional banks outside North America.
- **Merchant labels hide what you actually bought.** Bank feeds show `APPLE.COM/BILL`, `PAYPAL *DIGITALOCEAN`, or a bare dollar amount without distinguishing a 7-day free trial from a yearly plan that renews in eleven months.

## Moving your recurring bills off Copilot or Monarch in 15 minutes

You do not need to export years of checking-account history to move your subscription list elsewhere:

1. **Open the Recurring tab once in Copilot or Monarch.** Export or jot down only active subscriptions, software tools, domains, and trials—ignore one-off grocery or utility rows.
2. **Untangle hidden store charges.** Look up any `APPLE.COM/BILL` or `PAYPAL *` lines using your [Apple purchase history](https://steadyrenew.com/blog/apple-free-trial-charge-refund/) or [PayPal automatic payments](https://steadyrenew.com/blog/paypal-automatic-payments-cancel-find/) so each real service name has its own entry.
3. **Enter annual bills and active trials first.** Monthly charges hit your radar every 30 days anyway; annual plans and 7-day trials are what surprise people.
4. **Set reminder lead times by cycle.** Put 2–3 day alerts on trials and 14–30 day alerts on yearly plans before canceling your old budgeting subscription.

## FAQ

### Can Copilot or Monarch alternatives track subscriptions without Plaid?

Yes. Dedicated manual subscription trackers, spreadsheets, and self-hosted tools like Wallos never ask for Plaid or bank logins. You build the initial list from statements, app stores, or billing screenshots and control when items change.

### Will a manual subscription tracker find hidden charges in my bank account automatically?

No. Without bank access, no tracker can scan past card transactions on its own. Doing one quick [manual subscription audit](https://steadyrenew.com/blog/how-to-do-a-subscription-audit/) across bank statements, Apple/Google Play, and PayPal gives you a complete starting list in about 20 minutes.

### Is there a one-time alternative without another yearly app subscription?

Yes. Spreadsheets and self-hosted Wallos charge $0 in app fees, some mobile trackers offer one-time unlocks, and SteadyRenew includes unlimited subscriptions on its Free plan ($0) with an optional $9 USD one-time lifetime upgrade instead of recurring app fees.

### What if I track bills across USD, EUR, GBP, CNY, or JPY?

Because manual trackers record what each merchant actually bills rather than scraping a single US bank feed, tools like SteadyRenew store each plan in its native currency and convert monthly and annual totals into your chosen display currency automatically.
