A subscription audit is a one-sitting pass to list every recurring charge, decide **keep / cancel / remind** for each one, cancel what you do not need on the provider’s own site, and set reminders so annual renewals and trials do not surprise you again.

This process is sized for an afternoon. It works from statements, app stores, wallets, and email you already can open. You do **not** need to share bank credentials with a third-party app.

## Short answer

1. Gather statements and open a blank list (service, amount, currency, cycle, next renewal, decision, where it bills).
2. Check bank and card statements **and** Apple, Google Play, PayPal or similar wallets, and email renewals.
3. Look far enough back that **annual and quarterly** charges appear; confirm dates on the provider’s billing page.
4. For each item, decide **keep**, **cancel**, or **remind**.
5. Cancel on the provider’s site or app; save confirmation; verify the next bill does not appear.
6. Set renewal or trial reminders on everything you keep.
7. Re-check lightly when new trials start; do a fuller audit a few times a year.

## Why a one-time cancel purge isn’t enough

Cancelling unused plans once does not stop the next free trial from converting, or an annual plan you meant to keep from renewing in the background. Leave the sitting with a **living list** plus a reminder habit — not only a shorter credit-card statement.

## Before you start (15 minutes of setup)

### Gather payment sources

Collect every card and account you might have used for trials, including old cards that may still be on file. This is a read-only review of statements you already have. You do not need to share logins with anyone.

### Open a blank list (spreadsheet, notes, or tracker)

Minimum columns:

- Service name
- Amount
- Currency
- Billing cycle (monthly / yearly / custom)
- Next renewal (or `unknown`)
- Decision: keep / cancel / remind
- Where it bills (Apple / Google / card / PayPal / other)

## Step 1: Find everything (discovery checklist)

### Bank and card statements

Scan for memberships, software, streaming, delivery, cloud, and AI tools. Prefer a **longer window** than last month so annual and quarterly charges can appear. The point is coverage, not a magic number of months: if a charge only hits once a year, a 30-day view will miss it.

### Apple subscriptions

On iPhone: Settings → your name → Subscriptions. On Mac, use the App Store account subscriptions list. Family-shared Apple Account plans may not show on every device — check the account that actually pays.

Apple’s merchant name on a card statement often says Apple, not the app brand. Use Apple’s official help to view or cancel: [Cancel a subscription from Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118428).

### Google Play subscriptions

Open Google Play subscriptions for the Google Account that pays. **Uninstalling an app does not cancel the subscription.** Official steps: [Cancel, pause, or change a subscription on Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7018481).

### PayPal and similar wallets

Open automatic / recurring payments in the wallet and match each agreement to a line on your list. Forgotten PayPal agreements are easy to miss if you only read a card statement.

### Email search

Search for clues such as: subscription, renewal, receipt, invoice, trial, membership. Treat email as a **clue**, then confirm on the provider’s real site or app. Skip “manage billing” links inside renewal spam if you are not sure they are authentic.

### Optional: WeChat Pay / Alipay

If you also pay via WeChat Pay or Alipay auto-renew, add those lines to the same list. Detailed Chinese-platform steps belong in a separate guide; this English page does not walk through those apps.

## Step 2: Put every plan on one list

Deduplicate (the same streaming plan billed by Apple vs billed by the merchant). Note a **monthly equivalent** so you can compare, *and* keep the real charge date — banks do not debit the average every month.

Flag `unknown` renewal dates and look them up on the provider’s billing page before you decide.

## Step 3: Decide — keep / cancel / remind

Treat **remind** as a first-class outcome, not a leftover.

### Keep

Used recently, value is clear, and a cheaper free tier would not actually cover how you use it.

### Cancel

Unused, duplicate, or “just in case” with no plan to use it soon. Cancel on the **provider’s** site or app (Apple, Google Play, merchant, or wallet). This guide does not paste long UI scripts that go stale — use the official help pages linked above.

### Remind (pause the decision / trial / annual review)

Use remind when a free trial is nearing its end, when you want to keep an annual plan *for now* but review it before renewal, or when a shared household plan needs a conversation first.

## Step 4: Cancel carefully, then confirm

Use the provider’s own cancel flow. Save the confirmation email or screenshot. Then check the **next expected billing window** — a line on your tracker is not proof the merchant stopped charging.

For consumer background on free trials, auto-renewals, and negative-option billing, see the FTC’s [Getting In and Out of Free Trials, Auto-Renewals, and Negative Option Subscriptions](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-and-out-free-trials-auto-renewals-and-negative-option-subscriptions). That page is general consumer information, not legal advice for your situation.

## Step 5: Set reminders on what you keep

Practical defaults (not universal rules): about **one week** before a monthly renewal; about **two to four weeks** before an annual renewal or a trial end. A calendar app works. A dedicated tracker helps when you have many cycles or currencies and the calendar starts to drift.

## How often to re-audit

After the first full pass: a light monthly glance for new trials, plus a fuller review a few times a year (seasonal is fine). Re-audit when cards, household members, or phone accounts change.

## Manual list vs bank-linked tool vs simple tracker app

- **Bank-linked apps:** Fast discovery if you want a third party to read transactions. Fine if that tradeoff is acceptable. This checklist does not require that path.
- **Spreadsheet or notes:** Free and enough for a short list. Weaker when you need multi-device sync, mixed currencies, and renewal discipline.
- **Manual / privacy tracker:** You enter items once (or capture from a receipt or screenshot if the tool supports assisted entry). No bank credentials required. You still cancel with each provider.

A spreadsheet can be enough for a handful of lines. An app helps when the list should stay current across devices, currencies, and reminder dates. A full spreadsheet-vs-app comparison is a future page.

## FAQ

### How do I find all my subscriptions in one place?

You build one list yourself. Start with bank and card statements, then add Apple subscriptions, Google Play subscriptions, PayPal or similar wallets, and email receipts. Hidden channels are why a statement-only pass is incomplete.

### What should I check besides my bank or card statement?

App Store / Apple ID subscriptions, Google Play subscriptions, wallet automatic payments, and email search for renewal or invoice keywords. Confirm every clue on the provider’s billing page.

### How far back should I look for annual or quarterly charges?

Far enough that a charge which only appears once a quarter or once a year can show up. Then confirm the next date on the provider page rather than guessing from a single month.

### What fields should I record for each subscription?

Service name, amount, currency, billing cycle, next renewal (or unknown), keep / cancel / remind, and where it bills.

### How do I decide whether to keep or cancel a subscription?

Keep what you use and value. Cancel unused or duplicate plans. Choose remind when you need a trial deadline, an annual review, or a household conversation before you act.

### How do I cancel Apple or Google Play subscriptions?

Use each platform’s official manage/cancel flow. Apple: [Cancel a subscription from Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118428). Google Play: [Cancel, pause, or change a subscription](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7018481). Uninstalling an Android app does not cancel Play billing.

### How do I set reminders so I don’t forget renewals or trials?

Calendar alerts work. A common pattern is ~1 week before monthly renewals and ~2–4 weeks before annual renewals or trial ends. Put the reminder on the real charge date, not on a monthly-equivalent average.

### Do I need to link my bank to do a subscription audit?

No. A complete audit can be done from statements, app stores, wallets, and email. Bank-linked products are optional for people who want automated transaction scanning.

### How often should I re-audit after the first pass?

Glance for new trials monthly; do a fuller review a few times a year; repeat when payment methods or household accounts change.

### What’s the difference between a one-time cancel purge and an ongoing tracker?

A purge shrinks the current bill. A tracker keeps the list alive so new trials and annual renewals are visible before they charge.

### Can a spreadsheet be enough, or when does an app help?

A spreadsheet is enough for a short, stable list. An app helps with reminders, multi-device sync, mixed currencies, and a spend overview of what you entered.

### After I cancel, how do I confirm billing actually stopped?

Save the provider confirmation, then watch the next expected billing window on the statement or wallet. Tracker status alone is not confirmation.
